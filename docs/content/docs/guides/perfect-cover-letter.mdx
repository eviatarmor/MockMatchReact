A cover letter earns a read when it answers three questions: why this role, why you, and why now. MockMatch stores and exports letters; this guide is the craft layer on top of [Cover letters](/docs/documents/cover-letters).

## When a letter helps

Write one when:

- The application asks for it
- You are changing industries or titles and need a bridge
- A referral or recruiter expects a short narrative

Skip a long letter when the form only wants a resume and a one-line note. Respect the channel.

## Length and shape

Aim for about **half a page to one page** when exported. A reliable shape:

1. **Opening**: role title, where you found it, one sentence of fit
2. **Proof**: one or two short stories with outcomes that map to the post
3. **Close**: interest in next step; thanks; no pressure language

Three short paragraphs beat five fluffy ones.

## Voice

Match MockMatch product tone in spirit: calm, clear, specific. Avoid:

- Hustle clichés and empty passion claims
- Restating the entire resume
- Flattery about the company without a concrete reason

Use “I” for ownership of work. Prefer present and past tense that matches the story. Keep the employer’s language when it is accurate to your background.

## Target the role

1. Pull three requirements from the job post.
2. Map each to a proof point you can defend in an interview.
3. Drop anything that does not serve that map.

For a MockMatch-assisted pass, generate a **fit cover letter** from [Discover](/docs/jobs/discover) when available, then edit in Cover Letters until every sentence is true and specific. AI drafts are starting points; you own the final voice.

## Workflow in MockMatch

1. Create or import a letter in **Cover Letters**.
2. Draft with the role open beside you (or from a fit generation).
3. Use the AI rail for tone or structure rewrites on a selection, then fact-check.
4. Export PDF with the resume pair you will submit.
5. Track the application stage on [Applications](/docs/jobs/applications).

## Checklist before export

- Role title and company names are correct
- One clear reason this company or team (not generic)
- Proof bullets match the resume (no contradictions)
- No more than one page
- Contact path is obvious if the letter stands alone
